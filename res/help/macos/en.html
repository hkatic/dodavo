Dodavo Help

Dodavo sends files and folders directly from one computer to another. You don't need an account, a cloud service or a link: each of you has a Contact ID, and files go straight from one computer to the other, end-to-end encrypted. It works between Macs, and between a Mac and a Windows PC.

Using this help

This help opens in your web browser, in the language Dodavo is using. To open it again, choose Help > Dodavo Help, or press ⌘?.

Each topic is a level 2 heading. With VoiceOver, press VO-⌘-H to go to the next heading, or open the rotor with VO-U to choose from a list of them.

Names of menus, buttons and other controls are written exactly as they appear in Dodavo. Everything can be done with the keyboard alone and with VoiceOver; where that differs from using a pointer, the topic says how.

How Dodavo works

Every computer running Dodavo has a Contact ID: a code of 64 characters that takes the place of an address. You share it once; it stays the same, wherever the computer goes, until you change it yourself.

  1. You copy your Contact ID and send it to the other person, for example by email or in a message.
  2. The other person adds you to their recipients, and you add them to yours.
  3. To send something, you select the person and choose what to send.
  4. The other person's Dodavo asks whether to accept it and where to save it, or starts by itself if you are one of their recipients.
  5. The item goes directly to their computer, encrypted all the way.

Dodavo has to be running on both computers. Closing its window does not quit it, so that it can receive files in the background; Quit Dodavo (⌘Q) does.

Installing and uninstalling

Dodavo needs macOS 11 Big Sur or later on a Mac with Apple silicon.

Open the disk image Dodavo-<version>-macos.dmg and open Dodavo from it. It then offers to move itself to the Applications folder: choose Move to Applications Folder. You can also do that in the Finder without dragging: select Dodavo, press ⌘C, press ⇧⌘A to go to the Applications folder, and press ⌘V.

Dodavo cannot update itself while it runs from the disk image, which is why it asks Move Dodavo to the Applications Folder?

The first time they are needed, macOS asks whether Dodavo may use the local network, send notifications, and use folders such as Downloads. Allow them: see Troubleshooting for what happens otherwise, and how to change your answer later.

To uninstall, quit Dodavo and move it to the Trash. Your settings and Contact ID stay in the folder ~/Library/Application Support/Dodavo, so that a new installation keeps the same Contact ID; delete that folder as well if you want them gone.

Getting started

  1. Choose File > Copy My Contact ID (⇧⌘C) and send your Contact ID to the other person.
  2. Ask them to do the same, then choose File > Add Recipient… (⌘N) and enter a name and their Contact ID.
  3. With the person selected in the list, choose File > Send… (⌘O) and pick what to send.
  4. Follow it in Transfers: Transfers > Show Transfers (⌘T).

The main window

The main window lists your recipients in a table with the columns Name, Contact ID and Transfer. The last one says what is going on with that person. Below the table, a status line says whether Dodavo is online; VoiceOver announces when that changes.

The toolbar has the commands Send, Chat, Add Recipient, Copy My Contact ID and Transfers. Send and Chat are unavailable until a recipient is selected.

With VoiceOver, move to the table and press VO-⇧-↓ to interact with it, then use the arrow keys to move between recipients. Press Return to send to the selected recipient, or VO-⇧-M to open its menu, with Send…, Chat, Show Transfer, Edit Recipient…, Remove Recipient and Block Recipient.

Without VoiceOver, turn on Keyboard navigation in System Settings > Keyboard to move between all controls with Tab; macOS has it off by default, and Tab then reaches only text fields and lists.

Managing recipients

Add Recipient… (⌘N) asks for a name, which only you see, and the person's Contact ID. Ask the person to choose Copy My Contact ID in their copy of Dodavo and send it to you.

Edit Recipient… (⌘I) changes them, and Remove Recipient (⌘⌫, or Delete in the table) removes the person, after asking. Transfers that are running go on.

Block Recipient (⌘B) blocks the person, after asking: they leave your recipients, and from now on Dodavo declines everything they send you, without asking. They are not told that they are blocked; they see only that their offer was declined. Transfers that are already under way go on.

Copy My Contact ID (⇧⌘C) puts your own Contact ID on the clipboard. You also find it in General in Settings.

Change My Contact ID… in the File menu gives you a new Contact ID, after asking; Change… in General in Settings does the same. The current one then stops working for good, so everyone who sends you files has to get the new one from you and replace the old one. No transfer can continue under another Contact ID: Dodavo says how many are under way or waiting for your answer, and cancels them when you confirm. A send whose data has all been sent and that the recipient has not confirmed yet cannot be cancelled this way; while there is one, Dodavo says so and keeps your Contact ID. Your recipients and blocked senders stay as they are.

In both, the pop-up menu Largest item sets how large an item the person can send you: Same as for everyone, Any size, or This size with a size of their own, which comes before the limit in Settings.

Sending files and folders

Select a recipient and choose File > Send… (⌘O). In the panel you can choose files and folders, also several at once; each becomes a transfer of its own. Apps and documents that are packages count as folders here, and arrive as such.

There are other ways too:

  • Drag items onto a recipient in the table; a question comes first if there are several.
  • Drop items on Dodavo's icon in the Dock, and choose the recipient.
  • In the Finder, select items and choose Services > Send with Dodavo from the Finder menu, or from the context menu (VO-⇧-M).

To send the same items to several people, select several recipients first: hold ⇧ and press the arrow keys, or ⌘-click each of them. With VoiceOver, move to each recipient and press VO-⌘-Return. Then choose Send…, or drag the items onto one of the selected recipients. Each recipient gets a transfer of their own for each item. Edit Recipient…, Remove Recipient and Block Recipient are available while one recipient is selected. When items come from the Dock or from Services, the question of who receives them lists your recipients, and you can select several there in the same way.

A folder is packed automatically and unpacked again on the other side. Items that only mean something to macOS, such as .DS_Store, are left out. Links inside the folder arrive on another Mac; links that lead out of it are not sent. Finder tags and other extended attributes do not travel.

Windows does not allow some characters in names, such as : and ?. Since a sender cannot tell what system the other side runs, the folder carries names that any system takes, with an underscore in place of such a character. Another Mac restores the original names; a Windows PC keeps the changed ones.

Receiving files and folders

When someone sends you something, the Incoming Transfer window appears, a notification says so, and Dodavo's icon bounces in the Dock. It does not take the focus from what you are doing: click the notification, or the icon, to go to it.

The window says who sends what, and whether there is enough free space. What the transfers that you are still receiving have left to arrive is counted too. Save to: shows where it goes, and Choose… changes that. Accept takes it (Return), Decline refuses it (Esc). VoiceOver reads the window when you go to it.

When one person sends several items at once, they share one window, and your answer applies to all of them. The first line says how many there are, the next line gives their total size, and the Items table lists them. Items that arrive while the window is open are added to it, and those that arrived within the last second are not covered by your answer yet: they stay in the window.

Chat opens a chat with the sender, for example to ask what the item is before you answer: see Chatting with a person.

Block Sender (⌘B) declines the offer and blocks the sender, after asking: from now on, Dodavo declines everything they send you, without asking, and they see only that their offer was declined. The Blocked page of Settings lists the people you blocked, and Unblock lets them send to you again. Adding a blocked person as a recipient also unblocks them.

From people in your recipients, a transfer can start by itself: see Start transfers automatically, without asking in Settings. From anyone else, Dodavo always asks first, and says that the sender is not one of your recipients.

You can limit the size of an item that someone can send you: see Limit the size of an item that someone can send me in Settings.

Received items get the same mark as downloads from the internet, so macOS asks before opening an app that came this way.

Volumes in MS-DOS (FAT) format cannot hold files of 4 GB or more; Dodavo says so before it accepts.

Following a transfer

Transfers (⌘T) lists all transfers. Each row shows the item, the other person, what it is doing, how much is done, the speed and the time remaining. Closing this window does nothing to them.

With VoiceOver, each row reads as one sentence. Press VO-⇧-M on a row for a menu of what can be done with it, or VO-⇧-↓ to reach its buttons: Pause and Resume, Cancel Transfer, Chat, and when it has finished Open, Show in Finder and Remove from List. Changes in what a transfer is doing are announced while Dodavo is in front, and otherwise come as notifications.

On the keyboard alone, select a row with the arrow keys: Space pauses or resumes it, Delete cancels it, or removes a finished one from the list, and Return opens what was saved.

If the connection is lost, or a computer goes to sleep or restarts, the transfer waits and continues where it stopped once both are running and online. After 30 days without progress, the transfer ends. At the end, the whole item is checked.

While data moves, Dodavo keeps the Mac from going to sleep by itself. Closing a laptop's lid still puts it to sleep; the transfer goes on after it wakes.

Once all of an item has arrived, it is the receiver's, so the sender can no longer cancel the transfer. Clear Finished removes the rows of transfers that have ended.

Chatting with a person

You can exchange short text messages with a person, for example to say what you are about to send or to ask about something you were offered. Messages go directly from one computer to the other, encrypted, over the same kind of connection as files. Both of you have to be running Dodavo and be online at the same moment, and the Dodavo on the other side has to have chat too.

Opening a chat

  • In the main window, select the person and choose File > Chat (⇧⌘M), or Chat in the toolbar or in the person's menu.
  • In the Incoming Transfer window, choose Chat to write to the sender before you answer. In Transfers, each row has a Chat button for the person the transfer is with; File > Chat does the same for the selected row.
  • When someone writes to you, the chat window with that person opens by itself. It does not take the focus from what you are doing.

There is one chat window for each person. Its title is Chat with followed by the person's name, and the focus starts in the Message field.

Writing to several people at once

To send the same message to several people, select them in the list of recipients and choose File > Chat (⇧⌘M). Instead of a chat, Dodavo asks for the message: Message to Several People names the recipients and has a field for the message, with Send (Return) and Cancel (Esc). Each person gets the message as a private message from you. They do not see who else got it, and each answer arrives in the chat with that person. The message is added to your conversation with each of them. If it cannot be delivered to one of them, a notification says so, and that conversation marks it as not sent.

The chat window

  • Conversation: a table with one message in each row, the newest last. Each row says who wrote the message, when, and the text, for example “Ana, 14:32: The photos are on their way.” or “You, 14:33: Thanks!”. A message from another day also has its date. Read the messages with the arrow keys, and copy the selected ones with ⌘C. With VoiceOver, press VO-⇧-↓ on the table to interact with it; each row reads as one sentence.
  • The status line, below the conversation: the message that arrived last, that the other person is typing, or why a message was not sent.
  • Message: where you write. A message can have up to 4000 characters.
  • Send (Return): sends the message and adds it to the conversation. The focus stays in the field.
  • Add to My Recipients…: only for a person who is not in your recipients; it opens Add Recipient with their name and Contact ID filled in.
  • Block: blocks the person, after asking, and closes the chat. A blocked person can neither write to you nor send you anything.
  • Delete Conversation…: deletes the messages with this person from this Mac, after asking.

Closing the window (⌘W) keeps the conversation: it is there again when you open the chat.

When a message arrives

  • While the chat window is in front, the message is added to the conversation and shown in the status line, and VoiceOver announces it, with the name of the person first.
  • While you are elsewhere, the chat window opens or stays where it is without taking the focus, Dodavo's icon bounces in the Dock, and a notification, Message from with the person's name, shows the message. VoiceOver reads notifications as they appear. Click the notification to go to the chat.
  • A sound plays in both cases, unless you turned it off.

When the other person starts writing, the status line says so, for example “Ana is typing…”. While the chat window is in front, VoiceOver announces this once, with a short sound; it is not repeated while they go on writing. The other person is told in the same way when you write. Both can be turned off in Settings.

When a message cannot be sent

A message that cannot be delivered is marked in the conversation, for example “You, 14:35 (not sent): Are you there?”, the status line says why, and a sound plays. Dodavo does not try again by itself. The text is put back into the Message field if that is empty, so that you can press Return to send it again. The reasons you may see:

  • “The other computer could not be found on the network” or “Connection attempt timed out”: Dodavo is not running on the other computer, or one of you is offline.
  • “This person does not accept messages from you”: the other person takes messages only from their recipients, or has blocked you.
  • “Dodavo on the other side does not have chat”: the other person has a Dodavo without chat.
  • “The connection was lost” or “The other side did not confirm the message”: the connection ended before the message was confirmed.

Who can write to you

Unless you change it, anyone who has your Contact ID and is not blocked can write to you, as anyone can offer you a file. A chat with a person who is not in your recipients shows a warning with their Contact ID above the conversation. On the Chat page of Settings you can choose Only my recipients: messages from anyone else are then refused without you seeing them, and their sender is told that you do not accept messages from them. If you write to a person who is not in your recipients yourself, their answers reach you all the same, for a day after your last message to them.

Where conversations are kept

Conversations are kept on your Mac, with your settings, until you delete them: the newest 2000 messages with each person. They are not sent anywhere else. Delete Conversation… in a chat window deletes one, and Delete All Conversations… on the Chat page of Settings deletes all of them. Deleting removes the messages from your Mac only; the other person keeps their copy.

Running in the background

Closing the main window leaves Dodavo running, so that you can receive files. Click its icon in the Dock to open the window again.

With Show in menu bar on, Dodavo's icon in the menu bar shows how many transfers are running, and its menu has Open Dodavo, the running transfers, Copy My Contact ID and Quit Dodavo. With VoiceOver, press VO-M-M to reach it.

With Open at Login on, Dodavo starts when you log in; with Hide when opened at login, without showing its window.

Settings

Open them with Settings… (⌘,) in the Dodavo menu; before macOS 13 it is Preferences…. Changes apply at once.

General:

  • Your name: Shown to the people you send files to.
  • Your Contact ID: with Copy and Change….
  • Open at Login and Hide when opened at login.
  • Show in menu bar.
  • Check for updates automatically: once a day, Dodavo looks for a newer version, and shows its release notes when there is one. Check for Updates… does it at any time.
  • Language: System Default follows macOS. The new language is used the next time you open Dodavo, so Dodavo offers to do that at once, with Quit & Reopen (Return) and Later (Esc). If transfers are running, it says how many. Transfers that stop when Dodavo quits continue where they left off once it is open again; a large one may take a while to continue.
  • Folder for temporary files: where Dodavo keeps the ZIP archive of a folder while it sends it, in a folder of its own named Dodavo. Choose… picks another folder, for example on a disk with more free space, and Use Default goes back. The choice holds for transfers that start afterwards; while the folder cannot be reached, a transfer uses the default one.
  • Warn when a temporary file is too large for its volume: when selected, Dodavo tells you before a transfer whose temporary ZIP archive would be 4 GB or more while the volume it is made on uses the MS-DOS (FAT) format, which cannot hold a file that large. That is when you send such a folder, and when you accept one of that size for such a volume and keep only the unpacked folder. You can choose another folder for temporary files (Choose Another Folder…), go on (Continue Anyway) or cancel; Don't warn me again turns this setting off. Such a transfer is not accepted by itself. It is on unless you turn it off.

Receiving:

  • Always save received items to this folder, with Choose…. It is your Downloads folder until you choose another one.
  • Start transfers automatically, without asking. Only from people in your recipients, into the folder above. Without that folder, Dodavo always asks where to save.
  • When a folder arrives: Keep only the unpacked folder, Keep only the ZIP archive, or Keep both the unpacked folder and the ZIP file.
  • Limit the size of an item that someone can send me. Largest size: a whole number of MB or GB. The limit applies to each item by itself, from anyone; a folder counts by its size packed or unpacked, whichever is larger.
  • When an item is over the limit: Ask me before it starts (the item never starts by itself, and the Incoming Transfer window says that it is larger than the limit you set), Decline it without telling me, or Decline it and tell me (a notification says who offered what). The sender sees only that the offer was declined.

Chat:

  • Who can send me messages: Anyone who is not blocked, or Only my recipients. With the second, a message from someone who is not in your recipients is refused without you seeing it.
  • Tell the other person when I am typing: when selected, the other person's chat window says that you are typing while you write a message. It is on unless you turn it off.
  • Announce when the other person is typing: when selected, VoiceOver announces in the chat window in front, with a sound, that the other person has started typing. When it is off, the status line still shows it. It is on unless you turn it off.
  • Delete All Conversations…: deletes every conversation from this Mac, after asking.

Sounds:

  • Play sounds: Dodavo plays a short sound of its own for the events in the list. A notification about the same event then has no sound of its own. When it is off, no sound plays, and what each event is set to is kept. It is on unless you turn it off.
  • Events: a table of the events, each with what it plays: Message received, Message sent, The other person is typing, Message not sent, Incoming item, Transfer started, Transfer completed, Transfer failed, Transfer declined or cancelled, Connection lost and Transfer continues. Select an event with the arrow keys; the controls below apply to it.
  • Sound: Default (the built-in sound), None, or Choose… to pick a sound file of yours, such as a WAV or AIFF file. A file that cannot be played later, for example because it was moved, gives the built-in sound.
  • Play: plays what the selected event is set to, also while Play sounds is off.
  • Reset All: sets every event back to its built-in sound.

Blocked: the senders you blocked, with Unblock.

Connections and security

Dodavo connects computers with iroh (iroh.computer):

  • Directly, through routers and firewalls, without any port forwarding.
  • Within your local network, where computers find each other by themselves.
  • Through a relay server where a direct path is impossible. Data stays encrypted, so the relay cannot read it, but it is slower.

Everything is end-to-end encrypted, and only the two computers can read it. Each item is checked with a BLAKE3 checksum at the end. The row of a transfer says whether it goes directly or through a relay.

Your Contact ID is public by nature; what proves it is yours is a secret key in ~/Library/Application Support/Dodavo, readable by your user only.

Frequently asked questions

Can I send to someone on Windows? Yes. It works the same way in both directions.

Can the other person be offline? Not when a transfer starts: Dodavo has to be running on both computers, otherwise it says that it could not connect. Once a transfer has been accepted, either side may go offline, and it continues when both are back.

Does a file go through a server? Only through a relay, encrypted, if no direct path can be found.

Is there a limit to the size? Only the free space on the other side, and 4 GB per file on MS-DOS (FAT) volumes. You can set a limit for what you receive: see Settings.

Troubleshooting

macOS will not open Dodavo. Download the disk image again from the release page, and open Dodavo from it. The first time, macOS asks whether to open an app downloaded from the Internet: choose Open.

Dodavo says that it is offline. Check the internet connection. Transfers within the local network still work.

Transfers are very slow between computers on the same network. Dodavo may not be allowed to use the local network: check System Settings > Privacy & Security > Local Network. Without it, data goes through a relay.

A transfer says that Dodavo may not use an item. Allow it in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Files and Folders, and the transfer goes on.

There are no notifications. Check System Settings > Notifications > Dodavo, and whether a Focus is on. With notifications off, Dodavo's icon bounces in the Dock instead, and Transfers always shows how transfers are doing.

Something else went wrong. Send Dodavo's log to the developers: see Sending the log to the developers.

Sending the log to the developers

Dodavo keeps a log of what it does: its version and that of macOS, when it opens and quits, how each transfer and chat goes, and the errors it meets on the network and on the disk. When something goes wrong that this help does not explain, or Dodavo reports an unexpected error, the log helps the developers find out why and fix it.

The log contains no names of files or folders, no paths, no names of people or of your Mac, no chat messages, no Contact IDs and no IP addresses. The other person of a transfer or a chat appears under a name such as peer#4c1e9a, which changes every time Dodavo opens. A transfer appears under a number and a short random code that both computers write down, so that the two logs can be compared. The times are in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). The log stays on your Mac and goes nowhere unless you send it yourself. It never grows much beyond 2 MB: then its older half is removed.

To send it, choose Help > Show Log in Finder. A Finder window opens with the file Dodavo.log selected. Attach that file to a report on the Dodavo page on GitHub, with a few words on what happened and when, or send it to whoever asked you for it: copy it in the Finder with ⌘C and paste it into the message with ⌘V, or drag it there. The file is in ~/Library/Application Support/Dodavo (for a profile, in its folder under profiles there); TextEdit shows what it contains.
